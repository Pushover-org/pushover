## Project name

Proof of Claim

## Elevator pitch (≤ 200 characters)

A claims desk for class-action settlements: Claude reads every receipt, plain rules decide, two people
approve, PayPal pays, and unclaimed money goes to the court's charity.

## Inspiration

Settlement funds are flooded with fake claims: one administrator received 80 million fraudulent claims in
2023 (Jones Day), and 49% of 1.77 million claims in the Celsius settlement were fraudulent (FRT). Every
fake that's paid comes out of a real class member's share. Today's choices are to pay everyone, deny in
bulk, or ask a model to decide. We wanted a desk where AI does the reading, code does the deciding,
people approve the money and PayPal moves it, with every dollar traceable.

## What it does

- A claimant logs in with PayPal, so a claim belongs to one verified PayPal account and the money can
  only go to that account.
- Claude reads the receipt photo and returns structured findings. It cannot decide or pay.
- Twelve checks in code (class period, product, arithmetic, sales tax against the store's address,
  geography, duplicate images, template declarations, identity, velocity, visual anomalies, units, repeat
  receipts) feed a versioned decision table: approve, deny, ask for one more document, or send to a person.
- Linked claims (shared device, inbox or near-identical words) are held as a group; an AI investigator
  writes what it sees; a person decides.
- Money needs two people: an analyst proposes the exact round, a different supervisor approves, and only
  then does PayPal Payouts send it. Every item is read back from PayPal, matched against PayPal's own
  transaction history, and shown as unconfirmed until PayPal confirms it.
- Unclaimed money comes back after 30 days, is offered again, then goes to the court's chosen nonprofit
  in a second Payouts batch (cy-près).
- A public court report and ledger let anyone check every decision and dollar. AI assistants can file for
  people through an MCP server; the person confirms with Log in with PayPal.

## How we built it

- **PayPal:** Payouts (batch IDs that make retries safe; every item read back), Log in with PayPal
  (verified status and payer ID; payouts go to the payer ID), Webhooks (each delivery verified with
  PayPal before it's stored, and it only triggers a read-back), Transaction Search and Balances through
  PayPal's Server SDK (generated by APIMatic, configured with the APIMatic Context Plugin for PayPal).
  Sandbox only, enforced in the client.
- **AI:** Claude Haiku 5.5 reads receipts and writes the ring investigator's notes; Claude Sonnet 5.5 was
  the comparison. Findings follow a strict schema; spend is capped in code.
- **Pre-registered evaluation:** 157 labelled claims (real restaurant receipts from a public dataset, plus
  rendered and edited fakes) committed with the pass mark before any run, scored three ways: pay
  everyone, model alone, Proof of Claim.
- Next.js, TypeScript, PostgreSQL, a worker for read-backs and cy-près, Railway, AG Grid and AG Studio,
  Bryntum Scheduler, Playwright and Vitest (1,004 unit tests, 26 browser tests), an eval gate before every
  deploy.

## Challenges we ran into

- Our first idea (a guard for AI refund agents) failed our own pre-registered test: Claude agents were
  not talked into bad refunds (0 of 33 attacks). We stopped and moved to settlement fraud.
- On our first live payout, PayPal refused a batch ID our laptop had already used on the same sandbox
  app, and our retry-safe code adopted the old batch. No money moved twice; we now put each database's own
  name in every batch ID.
- PayPal's sandbox transaction history stopped refreshing for over five hours, so the app shows
  "waiting for history" rather than pretending a payout is confirmed.

## Accomplishments that we're proud of

On 100 held-out claims no rule or prompt was tuned on: **$40 of fraud paid, against $1,620 if everyone is
paid**, and **3 of 41 honest claimants wrongly denied** (the model alone denied 8). Claude recommended
approving and the rules said no 30 times; 28 of those were fraud or ineligible. A real sandbox round paid
a sandbox buyer's $10 to her PayPal account, and the unclaimed money is on its way to the nonprofit.

## What we learned

Letting the model decide is the weak point; letting it read is the strength. A fake with an impossible
sales tax fools the model and not the rule. And every honest miss we found is something a person can
review, which is why unknown never shows as safe.

## What's next

private demo copy per visitor, and fixing the duplicate-image false positive we kept and reported.

Claude Haiku 5.5 (Anthropic API) reads every receipt photo into structured findings and writes the ring
investigator's notes; it never decides a claim or moves money. Claude Sonnet 5.5 was run once on the
same held-out claims as a comparison. AG Studio's desk agent runs on Claude with read-only tools and answers
analysts' questions about the claims grid. PayPal's

written with AI coding assistance (Claude Code); see AI_ASSISTANCE.md.
